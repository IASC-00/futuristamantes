I. How we work

A lean shoot, a small post team, and AI only where it holds up.

We get the performances and the light in camera. AI cleans up, extends and alters what we shot. It doesn't replace the people in front of the lens. Every AI shot is planned before the shoot, not rescued after it.

  1. 01

    Breakdown

    Every shot in the script gets a tag: shot in camera, screen insert, altered, or generated. That list becomes the effects plan.

  2. 02

    Consent

    Written consent for any likeness, voice or altered version of a person, signed before anyone is filmed.

  3. 03

    Shoot

    Performances in camera. An empty frame after every effects shot, reference stills, and room tone at each location.

  4. 04

    Edit

    Proxies, assembly and cut. The picture is locked before the heavy effects work starts.

  5. 05

    Effects

    Removals, set extensions, screen replacements and altered imagery, each one from the plan in step 01.

  6. 06

    Sound

    Dialogue cleanup, replacement lines and the final mix. A synthetic voice only with the actor's signed permission.

  7. 07

    Color

    Grade, titles and finishing, shot by shot, by a person looking at every frame.

  8. 08

    Delivery

    Your film in every format you need, plus a log of every AI-touched shot: what changed, which tool, and what a person decided.

III. What we found

Where AI holds up, and where it still needs a camera.

Holds up in our tests

  • Wide shots with small figures moving through them
  • Screen replacements and security-camera looks
  • Color on calm, steady shots
  • A face held steady through a slow head turn

Needs a camera or a person

  • Adding a person into a shot: ours came out headless
  • Following direction: asked to turn and look back, the face kept turning
  • Fast action: color smears unless it's handled per frame
  • Stray detail: one model wrote nonsense text on a glass wall
